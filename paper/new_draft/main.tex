\begin{abstract}
Centralized multi-agent path planning with cooperative localization in GPS-denied environments is generally computationally 
intractable when formulated in full belief space due to tightly coupled uncertainty dynamics. Prior work either approximates belief dynamics
or restricts inter-agent coupling. We study a multi-agent planning problem in which a primary agent must reach a goal in minimum time 
subject to a chance-constrained collision-avoidance requirement and a terminal bound on localization uncertainty. We obtain a tractable surrogate
of the underlying belief-space problem by optimizing over the mean-state trajectories of all agents and propagating covariance for terminal constraint evaluation only. 
We propose a discrete-to-continuous pipeline combining hexagonal-grid joint search with spline-based trajectory refinement. 
Across 50 randomized scenarios, when the terminal uncertainty constraint is 50\% of the terminal uncertainty of a reference lone primary agent plan, our planner
finds solutions in 80\% of the scenarios against 54\% for the strongest baseline. We validate these results through the marine robotics simulator HoloOcean~\cite{potokar2022holoocean} and show that
the generated paths satisfy the collision constraint in 100\% and the terminal uncertainty bound in 93\% of closed-loop Monte Carlo trials.

\end{abstract}

\section{INTRODUCTION}
% ===========================================================================
%\budget{$\approx$550 words, 0.7 page incl.\ Fig.~1}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\figw]{fig1}
  \caption{The escort problem. The primary must reach the goal with its
    localization uncertainty under a terminal bound, and the support is routed
    to hold it there. Both trajectories are searched on the heading-aware
    hexagonal lattice and then refined as B-splines.}
  \label{fig:overview}
\end{figure}
In environments where GPS signal is unreliable, such as underwater, or in regions subject to spoofing or jamming, agents must rely on other forms of information
for localization via GPS-denied solutions. In GPS-denied environments, autonomous agents struggle with state uncertainty due to inertial measurement unit (IMU)
drift. As the estimation error accumulates, an agent's pose uncertainty can grow to a point where waypoint tracking degrades and mission objectives are compromised.
Common planning strategies ameliorate this by biasing trajectories toward informative landmarks and introducing cooperating agents to utilize inter-agent measurements for 
cooperative localization~\cite{roumeliotis2002dist,bahr2009coop}. Consequently, trajectory planning must account for uncertainty propagation when estimation error impedes task performance. While
belief-space planning provides a principled formulation for uncertainty-aware decision making, it is typically intractable in multi-agent settings due to the dimensionality of coupled
belief dynamics~\cite{kaelbling1998pomdp,platt2010belief}. This motivates surrogate formulations that optimize mean trajectories while propagating uncertainty only for constraint evaluation.

In this work, we consider a centralized multi-agent planning problem in which a primary agent must reach a goal in minimum time while subjected to a chance-constrained collision-avoidance 
requirement and a terminal bound on localization uncertainty.

We compute a coordinated discrete plan over a joint state space that captures motion constraints and inter-agent ranging capabilities. This solution is then lifted into
continuous space and refined into smooth, dynamically feasible trajectories that satisfy kinematic constraints.

Our contributions are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A formulation of centralized multi-agent path planning with cooperative
    localization (MAPP-CL) in the presence of obstacles, in which support
    agents are routed jointly with a primary agent subject to a terminal
    localization bound and chance-constrained collision avoidance under both
    agent and obstacle uncertainty.
  \item A discrete-to-continuous planner, a joint search over a heading-aware
    hexagonal lattice followed by B-spline refinement, that enforces the
    curvature, collision, and terminal-uncertainty constraints in both stages.
  \item A benchmark of 50 randomized scenarios against four baselines adapted
    from adjacent literature, on which the planner solves 80\% of scenarios at
    the 50\% constraint level against 54\% for the strongest baseline.
  \item Closed-loop Monte Carlo validation in HoloOcean with an independent
    factor-graph estimator, showing that the collision constraint holds in 100\% and the terminal
    uncertainty bound in 93\% of trials.
\end{enumerate}

In the following sections, we formalize the problem, describe the discrete and continuous planning stages and evaluate our approach in a simulated GPS-denied environment.

\section{RELATED WORK}
\label{sec:related}
% ===========================================================================
Planning under uncertainty is naturally posed as a partially observable Markov
decision process, which is intractable in general \cite{kaelbling1998pomdp}.
Belief-space planners restrict beliefs to Gaussians and propagate them along
candidate trajectories, over a roadmap \cite{prentice2009brm}, a tree
\cite{bry2011rrbt}, or a locally optimized path \cite{vandenberg2011lqgmp},
usually under the assumption that the most likely observation is received
\cite{platt2010belief}. These methods plan for a single agent. With several
agents the joint belief and the search space grow exponentially with the
size of the team.

Cooperative localization lets agents share position estimates to reduce
dead-reckoning drift \cite{roumeliotis2002dist,bahr2009coop} and is standard
in underwater navigation \cite{paull2014auv}. Because cooperating agents share
measurement history, naive fusion double counts information, and covariance
intersection \cite{julier1997cov} is the usual remedy \cite{carrillo2013ci}.
Planning with cooperative localization in the loop is more recent. Theurkauf
et al. \cite{theurkauf2025coop} grow a sampling-based tree over the joint
belief of a team, and Liu et al. \cite{liu2026risk} plan risk-aware
trajectories for underwater teams. Our support agents have no goals of their
own and are routed only to meet the primary's terminal bound, and our joint
search is exhaustive on a lattice rather than sampled.

Multi-agent path finding solves the discrete coordination problem exactly
with conflict-based search \cite{sharon2021cbs} or approximately by planning
the agents one at a time in priority order
\cite{erdmann1987multiple,silver2005cooperative}, but it treats positions as
known. Hexagonal grids reduce directional bias
\cite{duszak2021hexgrid}, and B-splines give smooth trajectories whose
curvature can be bounded \cite{feng2024bsrrt,karaman2011optimal}. Obstacle
avoidance under position uncertainty follows the chance-constrained
formulation of Blackmore et al. \cite{blackmore2011chance}, and we bound
each spline segment by its MINVO hull \cite{tordesillas2022minvo} so that
the constraint is linear in the control points.

\section{PROBLEM FORMULATION}
\label{sec:pf}
% ===========================================================================
Let $\mathcal{I} = \{1,2,\dots,P\}$ denote a team of $P$ agents, where agent $i=1$
is the primary agent tasked with reaching a goal location $G\in\mathbb{R}^2$.
All other agents $2,\dots,P$ are support agents whose sole task is to assist the
primary agent's localization. All agents move at a common constant speed $v$,
so minimizing time is equivalent to minimizing trajectory length, and we use
arc length $s = vt$ as a shared clock throughout. The environment contains $M$
static convex obstacles $\mathcal{O}_1,\dots,\mathcal{O}_M$, each defined as an
intersection of half-planes $a_j^{\top}x \le b_j$ with unit outward normals
$a_j$ and offsets $b_j$, and each located only up to a covariance $\Sigma_o$.
The environment also contains a set $\mathcal{L}$ of landmarks with known
positions $\ell\in\mathbb{R}^2$ and positional uncertainty $\Sigma_\ell$.

\subsection{Decision Variables}

We optimize over mean-state trajectories only. The decision variables are the
heading profile $\theta_i(s)$ of every agent and the primary's path length
$S_1$. Positions follow from the headings at the arc samples
$s_k = k\,\Delta s$ through
\begin{equation}
  x_i(s_{k+1}) = x_i(s_k) + \Delta s
  \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_i(s_k) \\ \sin\theta_i(s_k) \end{bmatrix}.
  \label{eq:dyn}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Covariance Propagation}

Let $\Sigma_i(s)$ denote the $2\times2$ position covariance of agent $i$.
Covariance is not a decision variable. It is propagated along the mean
trajectories and used only to evaluate constraints. Between measurements the
agents dead-reckon, and we model the drift as process noise whose variance
grows linearly with distance and is larger along the direction of travel than
across it:
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma_i^{-}(s_{k+1}) = \Sigma_i(s_k) + Q\big(\theta_i(s_k)\big)\,\Delta s,
  \label{eq:prop}
\end{equation}
where $Q(\theta) = R(\theta)\,\mathrm{diag}(q_\parallel, q_\perp)\,R(\theta)^{\!\top}$,
$R(\theta)$ is the planar rotation, and $q_\parallel \ge q_\perp$ are the
along-track and cross-track noise intensities.

\subsection{Landmark Measurements}

A landmark $\ell$ provides a range-bearing measurement whose quality decays
smoothly with the distance $d_{i\ell} = \lVert x_i - \ell \rVert$ through the
logistic taper
\begin{equation}
  q_{i\ell} = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\!\big((d_{i\ell} - r_v)/w_v\big)},
  \label{eq:taper}
\end{equation}
which is close to one inside the visibility range $r_v$ and rolls off over a
width $w_v$, with no hard cutoff. Every landmark contributes information
weighted by its quality, and the posterior follows the information form of
the Kalman update for a direct position measurement, identical to the Joseph
form for $H = I$:
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma_i(s_k) = \Big( \Sigma_i^{-}(s_k)^{-1}
  + \sum_{\ell \in \mathcal{L}} q_{i\ell}\,\big(S_{i\ell} + \Sigma_\ell\big)^{-1} \Big)^{-1},
  \label{eq:lm}
\end{equation}
where $S_{i\ell} = R(\beta_{i\ell})\,\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_r^2, \sigma_b^2)\,R(\beta_{i\ell})^{\!\top}$
is the sensor noise rotated to the line-of-sight bearing $\beta_{i\ell}$.

\subsection{Inter-Agent Fusion}

Agents exchange position estimates at fixed arc-length intervals $\Lambda$.
Their estimates share history, so adding the information directly would
double count it, and we fuse with covariance intersection
\cite{julier1997cov}, which is consistent under unknown cross-correlation.
When agents $i$ and $j$ communicate at separation $d_{ij}$, agent $i$
receives $P_{j \to i} = (\Sigma_j + R_c)/w_{ij}$, where $R_c = \sigma_c^2 I$
is the communication noise and $w_{ij}$ is a taper of the form
\eqref{eq:taper} with half weight at the communication range $r_c$, and
updates
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma_i^{+} = \Big( \omega\,\Sigma_i^{-1} + (1-\omega)\,P_{j \to i}^{-1} \Big)^{-1},
  \label{eq:ci}
\end{equation}
with $\omega \in [0,1]$ chosen to minimize $\det\Sigma_i^{+}$. Agent $j$ is
updated in the same way, and as $w_{ij} \to 0$ the transferred information
vanishes.

\subsection{Optimization Problem}

The planning problem collects the objective and all constraints as
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:opt}
\begin{align}
  & \min_{\{\theta_i(\cdot)\},\, S_1}\;\; S_1 \nonumber \\
  & \mathrm{s.t.}\;\;
    \det\big(\Sigma_1(S_1)\big)^{1/4} \le \bar{u}, \label{eq:term} \\
  & a_j^{\top} x_i(s) \ge b_j + r_a
      + z_\delta \sqrt{a_j^{\top}\big(\Sigma_i(s) + \Sigma_o\big)a_j},
      \label{eq:cc} \\
  & |\kappa_i(s)| \le 1/r_{\min}, \label{eq:kin} \\
  & x_i(0) = x_i^0,\;\; \Sigma_i(0) = \Sigma^0,\;\; x_1(S_1) = G.
      \label{eq:bc}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

\textbf{Terminal uncertainty \eqref{eq:term}.} The primary must arrive with
bounded localization uncertainty. $\det(\Sigma)^{1/4}$ equals the standard
deviation $\sigma$ of an isotropic covariance $\sigma^2 I$, so $\bar{u}$ is
in metres.

\textbf{Obstacle avoidance \eqref{eq:cc}.} Every agent must clear every
obstacle with probability at least $1-\delta$ at every instant, so for each
agent $i$, obstacle $o$, and arc position $s$ the constraint must hold for at
least one face $j$ of $\mathcal{O}_o$. The margin covers the vehicle radius
$r_a$ and the positional uncertainty of both agent and obstacle, with
$z_\delta = \Phi^{-1}(1-\delta)$ and $\Phi$ the standard normal distribution
function \cite{blackmore2011chance}. A union bound over the $PM$
agent-obstacle pairs bounds the instantaneous probability of any collision by
$\Delta = PM\delta$.

\textbf{Kinematics \eqref{eq:kin}.} Each trajectory respects the minimum
turn radius $r_{\min}$, where $\kappa_i = \mathrm{d}\theta_i/\mathrm{d}s$ is
the curvature.

\textbf{Boundary conditions \eqref{eq:bc}.} All agents start from known
positions with a common prior covariance, and the primary must end at the
goal.

The belief dynamics do not appear as constraints: $\Sigma_i(s)$ is a
deterministic function of the decision variables through
\eqref{eq:dyn}--\eqref{eq:ci}, left implicit in \eqref{eq:opt}. The agents
are coupled only through \eqref{eq:ci}, so the supports enter the problem
only by shaping the primary's covariance.

\section{METHOD}
\label{sec:method}
% ===========================================================================
Our planner solves \eqref{eq:opt} in two stages, a joint A* search over a
heading-aware hexagonal lattice and a continuous B-spline refinement of the
resulting plan, summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:joint}.

\subsection{Heading-Aware Hexagonal Graph}

A corridor of hexagonal cells of width $w_{\mathrm{hex}}$ covers the line
from start to goal \cite{duszak2021hexgrid}. A graph node pairs a cell with
one of six headings spaced $60^\circ$ apart, and three moves leave each
node: continue straight, or turn by $\pm 60^\circ$ and step into the cell
faced by the new heading. The heading changes by at most $60^\circ$ per
cell, which is the graph-level counterpart of \eqref{eq:kin}. Landmarks are
appended as sensor nodes without edges that enter the search only through
\eqref{eq:lm}, a goal node absorbs all six headings of the goal cell so that
the arrival heading is free, and obstacles act on beliefs during the search
rather than removing cells. All-pairs shortest-path distances are
precomputed on the graph and serve as the heuristic.

\subsection{Joint Discrete Search}

The first stage runs an A*-style search \cite{hart1968astar} over the joint
state of the team. A search state stores, for every agent, its graph node,
its covariance, and the arc distance travelled. Each expansion advances
every agent by one edge, so the successors of a state are the Cartesian
product of the agents' three moves. The accumulated cost $g$ is the
primary's arc distance alone, and states are expanded in order of
$f = g + (1+\varepsilon)\,h$, where $h$ is the precomputed shortest-path
distance from the primary's node to the goal. Supports contribute no
heuristic cost, so $h$ is admissible, and $\varepsilon > 0$ gives a weighted
search that trades a factor of $(1+\varepsilon)$ on the seed length for
fewer expansions. Ties are broken toward lower primary
uncertainty and then toward fewer idle supports.

During expansion each agent's covariance is propagated along its edge by
\eqref{eq:prop}--\eqref{eq:lm}, and \eqref{eq:ci} is applied whenever two
agents reach a communication checkpoint at the same arc distance. A
successor is discarded if any agent violates \eqref{eq:cc} along its edge or
if a support travels farther than the primary. Pareto pruning keeps the
joint space manageable: at each joint node signature the search keeps a
frontier of $(g, \Sigma_1)$ labels and discards a new label if an existing
one has no larger $g$ and a covariance no larger in the positive
semidefinite order. The pruning is sound because the heuristic is shared at
the same node tuple and a larger covariance cannot become smaller under
further propagation.

The search is anytime with respect to \eqref{eq:term}. When a popped state
places the primary at the goal and the spline seed it induces
(Sec.~\ref{sec:cont}) clears all obstacles, the state is returned if
\eqref{eq:term} holds and otherwise kept as the incumbent if its terminal
uncertainty is the lowest seen so far. If the expansion budget is exhausted,
the incumbent is handed to the continuous stage, which attempts to recover
feasibility. Only the terminal bound is relaxed in this way, never obstacle
clearance.

\begin{note}
Six headings quantize direction to within $30^\circ$, so a lattice path that
traces a smooth trajectory is at most $2/\sqrt{3}$ times longer than it. If
\eqref{eq:opt} has a solution with strictly positive constraint slacks, a
fine enough lattice therefore contains a feasible seed within that factor of
the optimum, and the admissible search returns one no longer than it. The
factor comes from the move set rather than the resolution, and removing it
is the job of the refinement.
\end{note}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\SetInd{0.4em}{0.8em}
\DontPrintSemicolon
\small
\SetKwInOut{Input}{Input}\SetKwInOut{Output}{Output}
\Input{hex graph $\mathcal{G}$, start states $x_i^0$, $\Sigma^0$, goal $G$,
       bound $\bar{u}$, expansion budget $N$}
\Output{B-spline control points $x$ of every agent}
$h(\cdot) \leftarrow$ \textsc{AllPairsShortestPaths}$(\mathcal{G})$\;
$\mathit{open} \leftarrow \{\text{joint start}\}$;
  $\mathit{inc} \leftarrow \varnothing$; $\mathit{seed} \leftarrow \varnothing$\;
\While{$\mathit{open} \neq \varnothing$ and expansions $< N$}{
  pop the state minimizing $f = g + (1+\varepsilon)\,h$\;
  \If{primary at $G$ and \textnormal{\textsc{SeedSplineClear}}$(\mathrm{state})$}{
    \lIf{$\det(\Sigma_1)^{1/4} \le \bar{u}$}{$\mathit{seed} \leftarrow \mathrm{state}$; \textbf{break}}
    \lElseIf{$\det(\Sigma_1)^{1/4}$ lowest so far}{$\mathit{inc} \leftarrow \mathrm{state}$}
  }
  \ForEach{joint successor (every agent one edge)}{
    propagate $\Sigma_i$ by \eqref{eq:prop}--\eqref{eq:lm}; fuse by \eqref{eq:ci}\;
    discard if \eqref{eq:cc} fails or a support outruns the primary\;
    insert unless dominated in $(g, \Sigma_1)$ at its signature\;
  }
}
\lIf{$\mathit{seed} = \varnothing$}{$\mathit{seed} \leftarrow \mathit{inc}$, the least-violating candidate}
$x \leftarrow$ control points of the seed splines;
  $\mathit{feas} \leftarrow \min s(x) \ge 0$\;
\ForEach{barrier stage; Adam step on $\nabla_x J(x;\mu)$}{
  accept iff $J$ decreases and ($\lnot\mathit{feas}$ or trial feasible)\;
  $\mathit{feas} \leftarrow \mathit{feas} \lor \text{trial feasible}$\;
  track least-violating and shortest feasible incumbents\;
  decay $\mu$ only once $\mathit{feas}$\;
}
\Return the best incumbent trajectory\;
\caption{Discrete-to-continuous escort planning}
\label{alg:joint}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Continuous B-Spline Refinement}
\label{sec:cont}

The seed is lifted into continuous space as clamped cubic B-splines, one
per agent, with the seed's cell centres as control points. Support paths are padded to the primary's node count, and
the primary's final control point is pinned to $G$. The decision vector $x$
stacks the free control points: the interior points of the primary and all
but the first point of each support, whose terminal position is free.

The constraints of \eqref{eq:opt} form a slack vector $s(x)$, feasible when
$s(x) \ge 0$: the terminal bound, evaluated by propagating
\eqref{eq:prop}--\eqref{eq:ci} along the sampled splines, the curvature
bound, sampled from the spline derivatives, the requirement that no support
spline be longer than the primary's, and obstacle clearance. For the latter,
each spline segment is enclosed in the convex hull of its MINVO control
points \cite{tordesillas2022minvo}, and each segment-obstacle pair is
committed to the face of \eqref{eq:cc} that the seed clears by the largest
margin, which fixes the homotopy class and replaces the disjunction over
faces with linear rows, one per hull vertex. The refinement then minimizes
the primary's length under the log-barrier merit function
\begin{equation}
  J(x;\mu) = S_1(x) + \sum\nolimits_k B\big(s_k(x);\mu\big),
  \label{eq:merit}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  B(s;\mu) =
  \begin{cases}
    -\mu \log s, & s > \delta_b, \\[2pt]
    -\mu \big( \log\delta_b + \tfrac{s-\delta_b}{\delta_b} \big), & s \le \delta_b,
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:barrier}
\end{equation}
where the tangent extension below the knee $\delta_b$ keeps $J$ finite for
violated constraints and supplies a constant restoring gradient, so one
objective serves feasible and infeasible iterates alike.

We minimize $J$ with Adam \cite{kingma2015adam} on forward-difference
gradients and a backtracking line search over a schedule of barrier stages.
While no feasible iterate has been accepted, as when the search handed over
its incumbent, any step that decreases $J$ is accepted and $\mu$ is held
large so that the barrier drives the violation down. The first feasible
iterate arms a latch: from then on only feasible steps that decrease $J$ are
accepted and $\mu$ decays between stages, shifting the effort from
constraint satisfaction to shortening. The least-violating and the shortest
feasible iterates are tracked throughout, and the better one is returned, so
the output is never longer than a feasible seed nor more violating than an
infeasible one.

\section{RESULTS}
\label{sec:results}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

We evaluate the planner on randomized corridor scenarios with one primary and
one support. Each scenario places the goal 600 to 900\,m from the start,
draws two to four landmarks with map covariances of 0.6 to 1.35\,m standard
deviation, placed 280 to 430\,m to the side of the direct line on
alternating sides so that every fix costs a detour, and draws one to three
convex obstacles of up to 220\,m across, centred on a triangular
distribution so that most scenarios have an obstacle on the direct line. The
lattice width is 150\,m, the minimum turn radius 40\,m, the visibility range
100\,m with a 15\,m taper, the communication interval 100\,m, and the
communication range 300\,m with a 20\,m taper. Dead-reckoning variance grows
at $q_\parallel = 0.25$\,m$^2$ per metre along track, a standard deviation
of 5\,m after 100\,m, and at one ninth of that across track. The obstacle
risk is $\delta = 0.05$ per agent-obstacle pair with a 5\,m vehicle radius,
the search runs with $\varepsilon = 0$ and $10^6$ expansions, and the
refinement runs 15 barrier stages sharing 1000 Adam iterations.

For every scenario we first plan a single unconstrained agent, which gives a
reference length $L_\mathrm{ref}$ and terminal uncertainty $U_\mathrm{ref}$.
The bound is then tightened from 100\% to 30\% of $U_\mathrm{ref}$ in steps
of 10\%, and every planner is run at each level. A planner that fails at one
level is counted as failing at the tighter ones. Scenarios in which a
straight line already meets the loosest bound pose no localization problem
and are discarded, which removed 34 of 84 draws to leave the 50 scenarios
reported here. Lengths are reported relative to $L_\mathrm{ref}$ over the
scenarios a planner solved, and wall-clock times are for whole Julia
processes including start-up and graph construction.

\subsection{Baselines}

We compare against four planners adapted from adjacent literature. All share
the covariance model of Sec.~\ref{sec:pf}, the obstacle test, and the
refinement of Sec.~\ref{sec:cont}, so the comparison isolates how the team
is routed.

\textit{Greedy.} The primary commits to its shortest obstacle-clear route
without uncertainty reasoning, and at every step the support picks, among
its three successors, the move that most reduces the primary's uncertainty
at that step, the myopic rule of greedy information gathering
\cite{krause2008sensor}. This removes lookahead from the support.

\textit{Formation.} The primary plans alone with a weighted A* over cells
and beliefs while the support holds a fixed slot in the primary's body
frame, one cell out at a $60^\circ$ multiple off its heading, as in
leader-follower formation control \cite{balch1998formation}. Goal candidates
are accepted on the uncertainty the primary has with the support attached,
and both lateral slots are tried. This removes support routing.

\textit{Sequential.} Prioritized planning
\cite{erdmann1987multiple,silver2005cooperative}. The primary is planned
first with the support parked at its start pose, under a bound slackened to
twice the threshold, the support is then planned against the committed
primary to minimize the primary's terminal uncertainty, and the primary is
re-planned once against the finished support path. This removes the joint
state space.

\textit{CL-GBT.} A port of the tree-growth strategy of the cooperative
localization guided belief tree of Theurkauf et al. \cite{theurkauf2025coop}:
one rapidly exploring random tree over the joint state in continuous space,
with goal-biased sampling for the primary and a bias that snaps the
support's sample onto the primary's. Its belief and measurement models are
replaced by ours and its iteration budget is set for equal compute time.
This replaces exhaustive lattice search with sampling.

\section{SIMULATION VALIDATION}
\label{sec:sim}
% ===========================================================================
Every planner in Sec.~\ref{sec:results} propagates the same covariance
model, so the benchmark cannot tell whether that model is calibrated. We
therefore fly a planned trajectory with a vehicle model, a controller, and a
state estimator that share none of the planner's equations, and check
whether the certified constraints hold over repeated runs with different
noise seeds.

\subsection{Simulator and Vehicle}

We use HoloOcean \cite{potokar2022holoocean}. Each agent is a torpedo-shaped
autonomous underwater vehicle with six-degree-of-freedom Fossen dynamics
\cite{fossen2021handbook}, driven by the simulator's depth and heading
autopilot at 30\,Hz at about 2.2\,m/s. An outer-loop waypoint follower
tracks the planned B-spline, resampled every 25\,m, with a 15\,m arrival
radius set by the turn radius, and it steers on the estimated position,
dead-reckoned forward between estimator updates, so estimation error feeds
back into the executed path. The planar field is placed on one depth plane
with the agents staggered in depth, so only agent-obstacle collisions are
under test. Three cadences are scheduled on arc length accumulated from
ground truth: the controller runs every tick, the estimator adds a pose node every 5\,m, and
an acoustic communication is attempted every 100\,m and succeeds only within
the planner's communication range. Measurements are ground truth with
additive Gaussian noise, so the simulation tests the consistency of the
belief model rather than the realism of any sensor.

\section{DISCUSSION AND LIMITATIONS}
% ===========================================================================
The discrete stage is complete only with respect to the lattice. The lattice
is laid over the corridor without regard to the obstacles, so the lattice
paths that clear them are neither a subset nor a superset of the continuous
feasible set, and a route that exists only between two cells is not found.
The refinement can shorten a seed and recover a small violation but cannot
change its homotopy class. A finer lattice narrows the gap at the cost of a
larger graph, and the all-pairs heuristic is cubic in the number of nodes.

The belief model is Gaussian and assumes that the planned trajectory
receives its expected measurements \cite{platt2010belief}. The simulation
tests calibration against an independent estimator fed ground-truth
measurements plus Gaussian noise, so Table~\ref{tab:mc} says the planner's
covariance is consistent with that estimator under the noise of
Table~\ref{tab:noise}, not that either models an acoustic modem or a sonar. The along-track bias in the terminal error shows that a consistent
covariance does not rule out a biased estimate.

The plan is centralized, computed offline, and executed open loop on a
communication schedule fixed in arc length; replanning after a missed fix is
not addressed. The joint search grows exponentially in the
number of agents, and all experiments use one support. In the benchmark it
cost 1.3 to 1.9 times the wall clock of the single-agent reference on
average, most of it fixed overhead, but the exponent will bind for larger
teams. Finally, the environment is planar with static convex obstacles of
known position covariance, and the vehicle is modelled by a turn-radius
bound alone.

% ===========================================================================
\section{CONCLUSION}
% ===========================================================================
We presented a centralized planner for a primary agent that must reach a
goal in minimum time in a GPS-denied environment while support agents are
routed to keep its localization uncertainty within a terminal bound.
Optimizing over mean trajectories and propagating covariance only for
constraint evaluation reduces the belief-space problem to a joint lattice
search followed by B-spline refinement, with chance-constrained obstacle
avoidance and a curvature bound enforced in both stages. On 50 randomized
scenarios the planner solved 80\% of the scenarios at half the reference
uncertainty against 54\% for the strongest baseline, and closed-loop Monte
Carlo trials in HoloOcean showed that the certified constraints hold on the
executed trajectories. Future work includes receding-horizon replanning,
scaling the joint search to larger teams, and hardware validation.
