
\begin{proposition}[End-to-end $\epsilon$-suboptimality]
Assume:
(i) A* returns an optimal solution on the discrete graph,
(ii) the discretization induces a bounded approximation error $\epsilon_{\mathrm{disc}}$ such that any continuous feasible trajectory admits a discrete approximation satisfying
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{disc}} \le (1 + \epsilon_{\mathrm{disc}}) L^*,
\end{equation}
and (iii) spline refinement induces bounded distortion $\epsilon_{\mathrm{spline}}$ such that
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{spline}} \le (1 + \epsilon_{\mathrm{spline}}) L_{\mathrm{disc}}.
\end{equation}

Then the returned trajectory satisfies:
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{returned}} \le (1 + \epsilon_{\mathrm{disc}})(1 + \epsilon_{\mathrm{spline}})\, L^*.
\end{equation}

Equivalently, for small $\epsilon_{\mathrm{disc}}, \epsilon_{\mathrm{spline}}$, we obtain:
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{returned}} \le (1 + \epsilon_{\mathrm{total}})\, L^*,
\quad
\epsilon_{\mathrm{total}} \approx \epsilon_{\mathrm{disc}} + \epsilon_{\mathrm{spline}}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
Spline refinement introduces additional suboptimality due to curvature enforcement and geometric smoothing, and should be interpreted as a bounded approximation operator rather than a cost-preserving projection.
\end{remark}
